# The five dimension animation rubric

The rubric evaluates a film and its editable source through five questions. It was developed while reviewing the butterfly film, then applied to thirty AAT-inspired proposals and the Tooth prototype.

| Dimension | Question | Evidence to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent | Does the audience receive the intended experience or meaning? | A screening without the accompanying explanation, audience responses and the relationship between setup and payoff. |
| Appearance | Do individual frames have coherent, readable visual form? | Composition, silhouette, materials, lighting, continuity and image artifacts. |
| Motion | Does change over time communicate action and intention? | Choreography, timing, transitions, anticipation, interruption, rhythm and coordination between sound and movement. |
| Delivery | Does the artifact reach its audience in the intended viewing conditions? | Playback, codec, sound audibility, captions, screen sizes, available downloads and actual listener experience. |
| Editability | Can the source be understood, revised and reproduced? | Editable representations, meaningful controls, dependencies, revision selection, reproducible builds, provenance and receipts. |

## Why these five

Each dimension can expose a failure while the others remain strong. A beautifully rendered repair may leave the repairer’s decision unexplained. Readable still frames may conceal weak timing. Expressive motion may reach the viewer with an inaudible voice. A successful film may depend on a source package that cannot be rebuilt.

Removing Intent loses the audience outcome. Removing Appearance loses the visual form of a frame. Removing Motion loses temporal expression. Removing Delivery loses the audience’s access to the work. Removing Editability loses the ability to continue developing it.

This is a practical minimal set for this workflow. The dimensions describe distinct questions, while particular defects can have consequences in several dimensions. There is no formal proof that the set is uniquely minimal, exhaustive for every art form, or empirically independent.

Sound illustrates the distinction. Whether a viewer recognizes his singing role affects Intent. Whether the jaw closes when the voice stops affects Motion. Whether the sung note can be heard on ordinary playback affects Delivery. Each judgment needs its own evidence.

## Scores

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | The capability is absent or fundamentally failing. |
| 1 | Major repair is needed. |
| 2 | The artifact functions with material weaknesses. |
| 3 | It meets the agreed standard within the tested scope. |
| 4 | Evidence shows that it exceeds that standard. |
| U | Evidence is insufficient for the particular check. |

Retain the complete profile in the order Intent / Appearance / Motion / Delivery / Editability. The ratings are ordinal judgments. Their spacing has no measured common unit. A failed requirement stays visible even when another dimension is strong.

## Three uses of the same rubric

**During discovery**, a concept gets forecast scores and a first experiment that could overturn them. Those scores describe expected potential and production risk.

**During production**, the artifact gets observed scores. The artist compares the delivered film and source with the brief and changes the weakest consequential part.

**During acceptance**, the review names the actual audience and environment. A single returning viewer’s approval supports acceptance for that commission. It supplies limited evidence about a wider unfamiliar audience.

## Ranking the thirty proposals

The preserved comparison contains all five forecast scores for every proposal. Its original recommended prototype order was 22, 1 and 10. Tooth offered a short test of emotional causality. Whale offered a strong continuous visual reveal. Octopus offered escalation through a readable mechanical rule. [Full selection discussion](concepts-archive.html)

The new catalog adds an ordinal Pareto-front comparison. Proposal A dominates B when A scores at least as highly in every dimension and strictly higher in at least one. Front 1 contains proposals with no dominating competitor. Remove that front and repeat to find fronts 2, 3 and 4.

This produces groups with genuine tradeoffs. Members of the same front remain tied by this comparison. The method supplies an ordering of fronts and preserves the five-score profiles. A production choice within a front also considers which experiment is affordable, decisive and creatively interesting.

The first front from the saved forecasts contains concepts 1, 10, 17, 19, 22 and 27. Tooth was selected for the first prototype because a 12–16 second test could reveal whether the audience understood the repairer’s reversal. This rationale survived contact with production: several technically valid cuts failed that test.
