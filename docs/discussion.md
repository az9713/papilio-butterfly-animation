# Discussion record and decisions

This is an edited record of the discussion about classification, concept discovery, evaluation and the Tooth production. The full saved thirty-concept proposals, justifications and score table are preserved in the accompanying archive. Personal source files and account-specific attachment links are excluded from the public record.

## From tool capabilities to a production loop

The opening questions concerned images, videos, animation and Blender integration through SVG & 3D Design, GPT Image and Blender. The working approach became generated visual references, editable construction, scripted animation and a review loop. The butterfly project established a complete film, source package and publication workflow.

Source reliability was tested through practical rebuild and revision scenarios. A valid film alone did not establish editability. Controls, explicit revision selection, safe resumption and source-only journal refresh all needed evidence. The final butterfly source release recorded acceptance within its declared Windows environment.

## The rubric preceded the AAT concept catalog

The request was for “a smallest set of exhaustive ‘orthogonal’ dimensions.” The result was Intent, Appearance, Motion, Delivery and Editability. The five questions separate audience purpose, frame quality, temporal expression, audience access and source continuation.

The later art-classification question introduced Getty AAT. Its terms helped expand the creative search. The project-authored experience-by-cause matrix supplied thirty different prompts. The existing rubric then supplied a consistent five-score forecast for every proposal.

This chronology matters. Getty maintains the vocabulary and its relationships. Our workflow supplies the discovery matrix, scores, justifications, tests and production decisions. [Getty explanation](getty-aat.md), [Rubric](rubric.md)

## Thirty concepts and the shortlist

The catalog crosses Discovery, Comedy, Suspense, Elegy and Liberation with Perception, Gesture, Material, Mechanism, Rule and Collective action. Each proposal has an AAT anchor, plot, rubric justification and first animation test.

The original top-three recommendation selected Tooth, Whale and Octopus. Tooth became the first prototype because the repairer’s reversal could be tested in a short film using one recurring phrase and a removable component. Its original question was “Why did the repairer take the working tooth back out?” [Original complete discussion and comparison](concepts-archive.html)

## Feedback that changed the film

| Feedback | Production consequence |
| --- | --- |
| “i dont see any repairer or customer” | Put both people at the same bench and connect the visible sleeve, hand and tool. |
| “there was no humming of the final D” | Replace the short synthetic cue with a generated vocal and give it a clear foreground moment. |
| “it is not clear why the tooth was removed” | Add his short line and her concerned reaction. |
| Later mouth openings produced “hissing” | Inspect the encoded sound and identify the filtered-noise breath cues. |
| “Show him trying to sing, being interrupted, then reclaiming the same note after removal.” | Rebuild the performance around three audible events and remove the synthetic breath cues. |
| “great. now it works.” | Record the requesting viewer’s acceptance of revision 5. |

The proposed cut-before-he-sings ending became an audible return of his note. The change answered the demonstrated comprehension problem. Revision 5 remained within sixteen seconds.

## What the failed checks taught

Earlier tests confirmed signal, frequency, words and complete playback. The feedback exposed missing or ambiguous dramatic meaning. The revision 4 assessment recorded 2 / 3 / 2 / 2 / 2 and named the weak vocal performance and unexplained payoff.

The accepted revision adds a clear sung ah, an interrupted fragment and an identical returned vocal. Tests caught lingering instrument notes under the attempt and dialogue; targeted level changes repaired those overlaps. The media, timeline, iteration journal and source receipts preserve that work.

The latest acceptance supports a 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 profile for the requested prototype. The only recorded audience acceptance is the returning requester’s response. A broader unfamiliar-viewer study remains open. [Evidence-based Tooth assessment](../projects/tooth/assessment.md)
