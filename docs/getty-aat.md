# Getty AAT and the creative search

Getty’s Art & Architecture Thesaurus, usually shortened to AAT, supplies controlled terminology for describing and finding cultural heritage. A record identifies a concept, gives preferred and alternative terms, supplies scope information and sources, and connects that concept to broader, narrower and related concepts. Its hierarchy can give a concept several broader contexts. Materials, techniques, styles, roles and types of work can therefore be combined when describing an object. [Getty scope and structure](https://www.getty.edu/publications/vocabularies-editorial-guidelines/aat-guidelines/1_about_aat/1.1/)

For example, a music box can be described by its object type, its materials and the techniques used to make it. These descriptions help a cataloger retrieve related objects and help an artist discover forms and processes worth exploring. Getty develops the vocabularies with contributions from cultural institutions and scholars. [Getty Vocabularies overview](https://www.getty.edu/research-conservation/tools-databases/vocabularies/about/)

## How AAT entered this project

The discussion began with a request for ambitious films using generated images, editable SVG/3D construction and Blender. The butterfly project had already supplied the five-dimension evaluation rubric. The subsequent question about organizing art led to AAT as a structured source of artistic vocabulary.

AAT broadened the subjects and processes available for new films. The rubric assessed whether a proposed film could communicate an experience and survive production. Their combination linked a search space to a selection procedure.

The five rubric dimensions were authored for this workflow. Their definitions and numerical ratings are project decisions. Getty’s cataloging vocabulary supplies the concept anchors and terminology. [Our rubric](rubric.md)

## From vocabulary to thirty films

We crossed five intended experiences with six causes of change.

| Experience | Perception | Gesture | Material | Mechanism | Rule | Collective action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| Comedy | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
| Suspense | 13 | 14 | 15 | 16 | 17 | 18 |
| Elegy | 19 | 20 | 21 | 22 | 23 | 24 |
| Liberation | 25 | 26 | 27 | 28 | 29 | 30 |

These thirty pairings are our creative matrix. For each cell we selected an AAT concept, invented a situation, specified a visible change and proposed a decisive animation test. Examples include anamorphoses for a whale assembled by viewpoint, music boxes for a repair that displaces a singer, and aqueducts for a chain of household decisions about sharing water.

The matrix encouraged variation in both emotional purpose and the mechanism driving the film. Its rows and columns are design prompts. A single film can still involve several experiences and several causes of change. “Orthogonal” describes an effort to reduce repetition across proposals. Statistical independence and mathematical minimality have yet to be established.

## The Tooth anchor

Concept 22 occupies Elegy × Mechanism and uses the AAT music-box record as its object anchor. The invented story gives a missing comb tooth a human consequence. An elderly customer has learned to supply its note. Repairing the instrument takes away his place in the tune. The repairer then decides to restore that place. [Music boxes, AAT 300042659](https://www.getty.edu/vow/AATFullDisplay?find=music%20boxes&logic=AND&note=&english=N&prev_page=1&subjectid=300042659)

The character, plot and emotional interpretation were developed in this project. The accepted film changes the original proposed breath-only ending into an audible return of his note.
